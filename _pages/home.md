## About me

I am a physicist in the Soft Matter and Fluids Physics department at the Institute of Physics of the Polish Academy of Sciences (IFPAN) in Warsaw. I earned my PhD from IFPAN in 2025, following my MSc (2020) and BEng (2018) degrees in Mechanical Engineering from the Rio de Janeiro State University (UERJ). Currently I'm a Visiting Scholar at Kyushu University in Fukuoka, Japan, working with <a href="https://www.zhenyingwang.com/" target="_blank">Prof. Zhenying Wang 王振英</a> on freezing droplets.

My research focuses on computer simulations of liquid droplets at the nano- and micro-scales. At these small scales, I use particle-based methods like many-body dissipative particle dynamics (MDPD) and molecular dynamics (MD). These techniques allow me to explicitly model the influence of different chemical species, such as surfactants. I am also developing a coarse-grained force-field for MDPD based on the molecular dynamics Martini force-field. Together with my collaborators, we have demonstrated the initial version's applicability by simulating lipid bilayer membranes and anionic surfactants.

During my MSc, I developed an in-house finite element Python code for fluid simulations. This code used the Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian method (ALE/FEM) to solve the stream function-vorticity formulation of the Navier-Stokes equations. That work gave me a continuum/CFD perspective on fluid simulations, which complements my current interest in fluids at the molecular scale.
